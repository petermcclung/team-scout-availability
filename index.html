How it is counted

What is checked, every minute
The sign-in page, an admin workspace address, Team Scout's own always-open registration form, an organization's public site, the server functions the mobile app calls, and the data service the web and mobile apps share.
A minute down
Any one of those six fails twice, 15 seconds apart, within the minute: no normal answer, the expected words missing, or nothing back within 10 seconds.
Availability
Minutes up divided by minutes measured, rounded down to two decimals. Months are Central time. Minutes inside planned maintenance are left out.
Major outage
Five or more minutes down in a row, or 30 or more minutes down in a day, when more than one check failed, or when the sign-in page or the data service failed on its own. These are the failures that stop people using Team Scout.
Partial outage
Any other minute down: one to four minutes, or a single check failing while the others answered.
Planned maintenance
A window posted on this page before it starts, with what it is for. Its minutes are measured as always but not counted. A window posted after it began is marked "declared after the fact", and the public record shows when it was posted.
Measured minutes
Minutes measured divided by the minutes in the month since full-strength measuring began on October 15, 2026. A minute no check reached is counted as not measured, never as up. The monitor's first two days, October 13 and 14, reached only part of each hour; they stay in the record and show hatched, and no minute of them counts.
Where the checks run
On GitHub's computers, separate from where Team Scout runs, so an outage on our side cannot stop the record being written. Every result is a commit in a public repository, so the record cannot be changed after the fact without it showing.
The record, the probe and its tests: github.com/petermcclung/team-scout-availability.
